Most AC breakdowns in South Lyon start with a small part: the run capacitor. It helps start the compressor and condenser fan, loses strength slowly over the years, and tends to give out on the hottest afternoon, which is why so many service calls begin with an outdoor unit that hums while the fan sits motionless. Other frequent culprits include contactors with pitted, burned points, fan motors whose bearings have run dry, slow refrigerant leaks that steadily rob capacity, and evaporator coils that freeze solid once a dirty filter chokes the airflow.

Warm air from the vents can mean a failed capacitor, a refrigerant leak, a frozen coil, or a duct problem, and each one comes with a very different price to fix. That is why South Lyon HVAC tests before touching anything. We meter capacitors against their microfarad rating, compare compressor and fan amp draws to the data plate, judge the refrigerant charge by superheat and subcooling instead of pressure alone, and inspect the coil, blower wheel, and filter whenever there is ice. Topping off refrigerant without locating the leak only buys cold air until the charge leaks out again.

Central air, heat pumps running in cooling mode, and ductless mini splits from every major brand are all part of our daily work. Because our trucks carry capacitors, contactors, fan motors, hard-start kits, and condensate pumps, most AC repairs are completed the same visit. Warning Signs for AC Repair

The sooner you notice these red flags, the easier and cheaper the problem is to resolve. Keep an eye out for the following clues that it's time to bring in an expert:

  • A burning smell or repeated breaker trips when the AC starts
  • Vents blowing air that is barely cooler than the room
  • A condenser fan that sits still while the outdoor unit hums
  • An AC that turns on and off every few minutes but never cools down the house
  • An iced-over evaporator coil or frozen refrigerant lines
  • Bubbling or hissing noises that point to a possible refrigerant leak
  • Water dripping from the indoor unit or showing up on the ceiling below
  • Cooling bills climbing while the house stays sticky and warm

Why Hire a Professional for AC Repair?

Air conditioning repair concentrates several hazards in one cabinet. Capacitors store a charge that bites long after the breaker is off, condensing units run on 240-volt circuits, and the refrigerant loop is a sealed, pressurized system that federal rules do not permit venting to the air; working on it takes gauges, a recovery machine, and a vacuum pump, not a can from the internet. The diagnostic traps are just as real: a frozen coil looks like a refrigerant problem but is just as often an airflow problem, and refilling a leaking system without repairing the leak wastes money while acid and moisture quietly damage the compressor.

Professional service sees the full picture, not just the symptom. Our technicians meter electrical parts before replacing them, make charge decisions from measured superheat and subcooling, locate leaks with electronic detectors and dye, and check the filter, blower, and static pressure any time airflow could be the issue. The result is a repair that lasts the season instead of a guess that fails in three weeks. If your system is not worth saving, you will hear it from us directly, backed by numbers rather than a sales pitch.

How to Prevent AC Repair Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Swap filters monthly while the AC is running hard to keep the evaporator coil from icing
  • Listen for new buzzing or clicking at startup, the classic sound of a dying capacitor
  • Leave vents open in every room, since closing them strains the blower
  • Keep shrubs two feet from the outdoor unit and gently rinse its coil each spring
  • Shade helps, but never enclose the outdoor unit; it needs free airflow on all sides
  • Clear the condensate drain when you first turn on the AC each year
  • Schedule a spring tune-up so weak parts are caught before summer arrives
