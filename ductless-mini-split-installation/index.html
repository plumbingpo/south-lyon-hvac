Think about the spaces your central system has always struggled with: a garage workshop that bakes by July, an addition the ductwork never reached, a bonus room that runs too warm or too cold, or an older South Lyon home built without ducts in the first place. Those are exactly the jobs a ductless mini split was made for. There is no ductwork to leak or lose energy, because each wall-mounted head is fed by a line set that passes through a small hole in the wall. Inside the outdoor unit, an inverter compressor modulates to match the load, so the room holds within a fraction of a degree, quietly and on very little power. And when winter comes, it runs as a true cold-climate heat pump.

Good ductless comfort starts with a load calculation, not a catalog. We size each zone to what that room actually needs, since an oversized head short-cycles, leaves the air damp, and never quite holds temperature. With multiple zones, the engineering gets stricter: capacity has to be split sensibly, and line set length and elevation change must stay inside the manufacturer's tables or capacity quietly suffers. Style matters too, because a wall head, a ceiling cassette, and a low floor unit each change both airflow and the look of the room. Our experienced technicians review every room in person, agree on placements that work physically and visually, and plan line set routes that stay hidden where possible and neat where they show.

Our installation process is held to the same standard as our biggest central systems. Nitrogen goes into the line set first to prove it holds pressure, then a vacuum pump pulls it down to a level confirmed with a micron gauge. Flares are torqued and leak-tested one by one, since that joint is the classic failure point. Drainage is set up with real fall or a quiet condensate pump, and the refrigerant charge is adjusted to match the line length. Finally, every zone is commissioned in both modes and we show you how the remote actually works. Why Hire a Professional for Ductless Mini Split Installation?

Because a mini split looks like a simple box on a wall, a lot of them get installed like one, and they fail in predictable ways. Flare connections are the weak link. Form one badly or leave it under-torqued and it seeps refrigerant slowly, so nobody notices until the head is blowing room-temperature air the next season. Skip the nitrogen test and the measured vacuum, and moisture stays in a circuit that inverter compressors do not forgive. Push the line set past the manufacturer's length or lift limits and you lose capacity without any warning. Forget the drain slope and the first muggy week leaves a stain down the wall.

Sizing discipline matters even more here than with central systems, because the smallest available heads are already generous for many bedrooms; the wrong pick short-cycles, leaves humidity behind, and never settles. Our experienced technicians size each zone honestly, torque and leak-test every flare, pressure-test and evacuate to a measured vacuum, set charge adjustments to the line length, and commission each head in both modes. Refrigerant work is federally regulated, and the electrical side needs a properly sized circuit and disconnect. The result is what a mini split should be: silent, efficient, and uneventful for fifteen years, instead of a mysterious underperformer from a rushed weekend install.

How to Prevent Ductless Mini Split Installation Problems

A little routine maintenance goes a long way toward avoiding a mid-season breakdown. Follow these tips from our experienced technicians:

  • Let the inverter do its job: pick a temperature and leave it rather than toggling on and off
  • Book annual professional service to verify charge, flares, and drainage while fixes are cheap
  • Have the indoor blower wheel and coil professionally cleaned before they grow anything
  • Brief defrost cycles and a little steam in winter are normal, but a unit encased in ice needs service
  • Glance at the condensate drain outlet in summer to confirm it is actually dripping
  • Clear snow, leaves, and clippings away from the outdoor unit year-round
  • Pull and rinse the indoor filter screens monthly, because they fill up much faster than you would expect
