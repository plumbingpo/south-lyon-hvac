A modern furnace runs a strict startup sequence, and knowing where the sequence breaks is most of the diagnosis. The thermostat calls, the inducer motor spins up to prove draft through a pressure switch, the hot surface igniter glows, the gas valve opens, the burners light, the flame sensor confirms fire, and only then does the blower deliver heat to the house. In South Lyon, MI, the failures cluster at predictable points: a flame sensor coated in oxide that can no longer prove the flame, a hot surface igniter cracked from years of thermal cycling, a pressure switch that will not close because the inducer is weak or the condensate trap on a high-efficiency model has clogged, or a control board with a burned relay. Each one stops the sequence cold, and each one mimics the others from the hallway.

South Lyon HVAC repairs furnaces by testing the sequence, not guessing at it. Our experienced technicians verify each stage with a meter rather than a hunch: igniter resistance, flame signal in microamps, pressure switch operation against actual inducer draft, gas valve voltage, and blower and capacitor health. Every visit includes the safety fundamentals on a fuel-burning appliance: heat exchanger inspection, leak-testing of gas connections, and flue draft verification, because the difference between a furnace problem and a furnace hazard is not visible from the thermostat. We measure temperature rise against the data plate before we leave so the fire and the airflow are matched the way the manufacturer intended.

South Lyon HVAC services natural gas, propane, and electric furnaces of every major brand, from standard 80 percent units to condensing high-efficiency models with their traps, drain lines, and dual pressure switches. Our trucks carry the parts that actually fail, such as igniters, flame sensors, pressure switches, capacitors, inducer assemblies, and blower motors, so a typical no-heat call ends with the house warming up. Warning Signs for Furnace Repair

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Booming or banging at light-off from delayed ignition
  • The igniter glows and clicks, yet no flame ever appears
  • Air moving through the vents that never gets warm
  • Flames that start, last a few seconds, and go out again and again
  • Water, rust stains, or black soot around the flue or cabinet
  • Short cycling every few minutes without reaching the setpoint
  • Yellow, wavering burner flames where they should be sharp blue
  • Grinding or high-pitched squeals from the blower

Why Professional Furnace Repair Matters

Gas, open flame, high voltage, and a flue all share one cabinet in a furnace, and the most dangerous failure is the one you cannot see: a damaged heat exchanger that lets combustion gases into the air the blower circulates. Every morning that delayed ignition keeps booming at startup, that exchanger takes more stress. The diagnostic side has its own pitfalls. When a furnace trips its high limit, the switch itself may be bad, or the furnace may be protecting itself from a clogged filter or a failing blower. Replacing the switch when the real issue is airflow removes a safety device and leaves the actual problem in place.

Our experienced technicians treat every symptom as a question about the whole system. Flame signal gets measured rather than assumed, so a healthy sensor is never replaced while a cracked igniter or weak gas valve skates by. Draft is verified at the pressure switch and the flue. Temperature rise is checked against the data plate, which is how airflow problems get caught before they shorten the exchanger's life. And the gas side is leak-tested with instruments, not a sniff and a shrug. The result is a furnace that has been proven safe and correct under a full cycle, and an honest report if the unit is nearing the point where replacement beats another repair.

How to Prevent Furnace Repair Problems

A few simple habits now, like regular filter changes and seasonal tune-ups, can add years to your system's life. Put these technician-approved steps to work around your property:

  • Store laundry supplies, especially chlorine products, well away from the furnace closet
  • Replace the filter every month while the furnace is working hard, since it costs less than any other part
  • Schedule service each fall to catch worn igniters and dirty sensors before cold weather arrives
  • On high-efficiency models, keep the condensate trap and drain line clear and draining
  • Notice how the furnace sounds at startup, because a quiet light-off is healthy and a boom is not
  • Put a carbon monoxide detector on every level and test them monthly
  • Give the furnace room to breathe, since a crowded utility space starves the burners of air
